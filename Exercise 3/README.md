## About the Data

### Data Source

The dataset used in this project contains information about **television models and their energy consumption characteristics**, including power usage, screen size, technology type, and efficiency ratings.

The dataset was provided as part of the course materials.

The dataset was provided as part of the course materials and originates from the **Australian Government's Energy Rating Database**, which is publicly available for consumer education and research purposes.

### Data Processing

Before creating visualisations, the dataset was processed to ensure it was suitable for analysis. This included:

- **Cleaning**: Removing or correcting missing values and inconsistent entries
- **Filtering**: Selecting only models available in Australia for relevance
- **Transformation**: Converting screen sizes from centimeters to inches (multiplying by 0.393701)
- **Categorisation**: Grouping screen sizes into categories (Small <43", Medium 44-65", Large >65")
- **Aggregation**: Calculating mean energy consumption for each brand and size category

The processed data was then exported for use in the web visualisations.


### Privacy

The dataset does not contain any **personal or sensitive information**. It focuses solely on product specifications and energy consumption data related to television devices. No individual consumer data is included, and all information is aggregated at the product model level.

### Accuracy and Limitations

While the dataset provides useful information about TV energy consumption, there are some limitations:

- **Completeness**: The dataset may not include **all available television models** currently on the market
- **Timeliness**: Some information may be **outdated or incomplete** as manufacturers regularly release new models
- **Real-world variation**: Energy consumption may vary depending on **real-world usage conditions** such as brightness settings, content type, and viewing habits
- **Self-reporting**: Data is self-reported by manufacturers, which may introduce bias
- **Testing conditions**: Laboratory test conditions may not reflect typical home usage

These factors should be considered when interpreting the visualisations.

### Ethics

When presenting data visualisations, it is important to ensure that the information is represented **accurately and responsibly**.

This project follows ethical data visualisation practices by:

- **Avoiding misleading visual representations** such as truncated axes or manipulated scales
- **Clearly explaining the context of the data** so viewers understand what they are seeing
- **Presenting information transparently** so viewers can interpret the results correctly
- **Acknowledging limitations** so viewers understand the scope of the analysis
- **Not overstating conclusions** or making claims beyond what the data supports
- **Using clear, accessible language** so the visualisations are understandable to all audiences
